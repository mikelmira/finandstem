## The short answer

There is no single way to plant an aquarium, because different plants grow in different ways. Push [stem plants](/glossary) and [rosette plants](/glossary) into the substrate one at a time with tweezers, keeping the crown at the surface. Glue or tie [epiphytes](/glossary) like anubias and java fern to hardscape and never bury the rhizome. Rest bulbs on top of the substrate. Tie or glue mosses to wood and rock. Do all of it while the tank is filled just above the substrate, so the water is shallow, the plants stay put and you can see what you are doing. Get the technique right for each type and almost everything else about planted tanks gets easier.

## Before you plant

New plants arrive in a few forms, and each needs a quick prep.

- **Potted plants** in rock wool: pop the plastic pot off and gently tease the rock wool out of the roots. It can trap waste and rot if left in.
- **Tissue-culture cups:** rinse the gel off the roots and separate the clump into many small portions.
- **Bunched stems** held by a lead weight: remove the weight and the band, and split the bunch into individual stems.

Always plant while the tank is only shallow-filled, an inch or two of water above the substrate. Damp and shallow keeps the substrate in place and the plants visible.

## Stem plants: one at a time

Stem plants grow as cut stems that root where they are planted. The rule is to plant each stem individually, not as a bunch, so light and water reach every one.

<SpeciesCard slug="rotala-rotundifolia" />

Strip the leaves off the bottom centimetre of each stem, grip it low with [tweezers](/glossary), and push it a couple of centimetres into the substrate at a slight angle. Space stems a finger-width apart and let them grow into a bush. If they keep floating up, you planted too shallow, push a touch deeper and let the new roots anchor them.

## Rosette plants: keep the crown clear

Rosette plants like crypts and swords grow from a central crown. Bury the roots, but keep the crown, where the leaves emerge, at or just above the substrate. Bury the crown and it rots.

<SpeciesCard slug="cryptocoryne-wendtii" />

These are heavy root feeders, so push a [root tab](/glossary) into the substrate near the roots, or plant them in [aquasoil](/substrates). Do not panic if a crypt melts after planting, it regrows from the roots.

## Epiphytes: attach, never bury

Anubias, java fern and bucephalandra are epiphytes: they grow from a horizontal rhizome and take nutrients from the water, not the substrate. Burying the rhizome kills them.

<SpeciesCard slug="anubias-nana" />

<SpeciesCard slug="java-fern" />

Attach them to [hardscape](/hardscape) instead. Two ways:

- **Glue:** a dab of cyanoacrylate gel (superglue gel) on the rhizome or a bare root, press onto dry-ish wood or stone, hold a few seconds. Safe underwater.
- **Thread or cotton:** tie the rhizome to the hardscape with fishing line or cotton thread; the plant grips on within weeks and you can remove cotton once it does.

Keep the rhizome fully exposed. See [attaching plants to driftwood and rock](/guides/attaching-plants-to-driftwood-and-rock) for the full method.

## Bulbs, carpets and mosses

<SpeciesCard slug="red-tiger-lotus" />

**Bulbs** (like the tiger lotus) are simply rested on top of the substrate or half-buried with the growing tip exposed, never fully buried. They sprout in a week or two.

<SpeciesCard slug="monte-carlo" />

**Carpets** are planted as many tiny portions pushed into damp substrate a couple of centimetres apart. Small and dense fills in fastest, see [how to grow a carpet](/guides/how-to-grow-aquarium-carpet).

**Mosses** are tied or glued in a thin layer over wood and stone. A thin layer attaches and grows out; a thick clump rots underneath.

## The mistakes that kill new plants

- **Burying the rhizome** of an epiphyte, or the **crown** of a rosette plant. Both rot.
- **Leaving rock wool** in the roots, where it traps waste.
- **Planting stems in a tight bunch**, which shades and rots the inner stems.
- **Planting into a full tank**, where the substrate clouds and plants float away.
- **Expecting instant results.** Most plants [melt](/guides/why-are-my-aquarium-plants-melting) a little as they convert from farmed emersed growth to submerged. New growth follows.
