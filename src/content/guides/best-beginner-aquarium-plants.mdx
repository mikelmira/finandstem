## The short answer

The best beginner plants share one trait: they tolerate neglect. The hardiest of the lot are the rhizome epiphytes (anubias, java fern), which attach to hardscape and feed from the water, and the hardy root feeders and stems (cryptocoryne, vallisneria, amazon sword, hornwort) that grow in almost any conditions. None of them need CO2 or bright light. Pick a handful of these, plant them thickly, give them six to eight hours of modest light, and you have a green tank that is genuinely hard to kill. Avoid demanding carpets and red colour-line stems until you have a tank with CO2 and more light.

## What makes a plant "easy"

An easy aquarium plant does three things: it grows in low to medium light, it does not need CO2 injection, and it forgives inconsistent care. Most easy plants come from shaded, slow-flowing streams, so they are used to dim, gentle conditions. The trade-off is speed: easy plants grow slowly, which is exactly why they are forgiving. Fast, demanding plants punish any gap in light, CO2 or nutrients by melting or handing their share to [algae](/algae).

## The hardy shortlist

### Anubias (barteri, nana and friends)

<SpeciesCard slug="anubias-nana" />

The single most beginner-proof plant there is. It is a rhizome epiphyte: you glue or tie it to wood or stone and never bury the rhizome, or it rots. It grows so slowly it almost never needs trimming and tolerates deep shade. The perfect midground accent on driftwood.

### Java Fern

<SpeciesCard slug="java-fern" />

The other classic epiphyte, attached the same way and just as tough. It grows a little faster than anubias and sprouts baby plants on its leaf tips that you can snap off and re-attach. Several cultivars exist (Windelov, Trident, Narrow Leaf) with identical care.

### Cryptocoryne wendtii

<SpeciesCard slug="cryptocoryne-wendtii" />

A superb low-light rosette plant for the midground, in green, brown and reddish varieties. Its one quirk is "crypt melt": it often drops all its leaves after being moved, then regrows from the roots within weeks. Do not throw it out when this happens. It is a heavy root feeder, so give it aquasoil or a [root tab](/glossary).

### Vallisneria and Amazon Sword

<SpeciesCard slug="vallisneria-spiralis" />

<SpeciesCard slug="amazon-sword" />

Two hardy background plants for filling space fast. Vallisneria sends up tall grassy blades and spreads by runners into a jungle back wall. The amazon sword is a big rosette that becomes a centrepiece. Both are hungry root feeders that reward a root tab, and both show [potassium pinholes](/deficiencies/potassium-deficiency) clearly if you under-dose, which makes them useful health gauges.

### Hornwort and mosses

<SpeciesCard slug="hornwort" />

<SpeciesCard slug="java-moss" />

For instant coverage, floating or loosely planted stems like hornwort grow like weeds and soak up excess nutrients, which is a real help in a new tank fighting algae. Mosses attach to anything and never need planting. Both are close to unkillable.

## Where each plant goes

- **Foreground:** low crypts, and easy carpets like [Monte Carlo](/plants/monte-carlo) once you are ready for a little more light.
- **Midground:** anubias and java fern on hardscape, cryptocoryne.
- **Background:** vallisneria, amazon sword, tall stems, hornwort.

Plant heavily and mix textures. See [how to plant an aquarium](/guides/how-to-plant-an-aquarium) for the technique, and [aquascaping design](/aquascaping-design) for arranging them well.

## Why beginner plants die (and how to stop it)

- **Buried rhizomes.** Anubias, java fern and bucephalandra rot if their rhizome is under the substrate. Keep it exposed and attach the plant to hardscape.
- **"Melt" mistaken for death.** Most plants are farmed [emersed](/glossary) and shed those leaves when they convert to underwater growth. New growth follows. Read [why plants melt](/guides/why-are-my-aquarium-plants-melting).
- **Non-aquatic plants.** Some shops sell true houseplants as aquarium plants. They rot underwater within weeks. Stick to known aquatic species.
- **Starving root feeders.** Swords, crypts and vallisneria in bare sand slowly starve. Add root tabs or use aquasoil.
- **Deficiencies.** Yellowing, holes or pale new growth mean a nutrient shortage, not a lost cause. Diagnose it in the [deficiency guide](/deficiencies).
