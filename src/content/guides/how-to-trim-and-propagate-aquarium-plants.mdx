## The short answer

Trimming is not just tidying, it is how you keep plants dense and how you multiply them for free. Each plant type is trimmed and propagated a bit differently: cut and **replant** stem tops, **divide** the rhizomes of epiphytes, snip **runners** off carpets and grasses, remove old outer leaves of rosettes, and **trim mosses flat** to make them branch. Trim little and often with sharp scissors, replant or share the cuttings, and a handful of plants becomes a whole tank over a season. Trimming also keeps light and flow reaching the lower growth, which prevents the leggy, algae-collecting mess that untrimmed plants become.

## Stem plants: cut and replant

Stem plants are the easiest to trim and propagate, and the most rewarding.

<SpeciesCard slug="rotala-rotundifolia" />

To trim, cut the stem a few centimetres above the substrate with sharp scissors. The stump sprouts two or more new shoots from the cut point, so the plant comes back bushier each time, this is how you build a dense stem "bush". The top you cut off is a ready-made new plant: strip the leaves off its bottom centimetre and push it into the substrate, where it roots within a week or two. A few stems become a tankful this way. See [how to plant an aquarium](/guides/how-to-plant-an-aquarium) for the replanting technique.

Trim little and often. A single drastic chop removes a lot of plant mass at once, which can trigger an [algae](/algae) bloom as the tank's balance shifts.

## Carpets and runners: snip and spread

Carpet plants and grasses spread by **runners**, horizontal shoots that sprout new plantlets.

<SpeciesCard slug="monte-carlo" />

<SpeciesCard slug="vallisneria-spiralis" />

Let them run to fill in a [carpet](/guides/how-to-grow-aquarium-carpet) or a jungle back wall, or snip a runner and replant the plantlet elsewhere. Trimming a carpet flat with scissors (or a trimmer) pushes it to spread sideways and thicken instead of stacking up and shading itself out. Vallisneria you thin by pulling whole plantlets, not by cutting the blade tips, which just leaves brown ends.

## Epiphytes: divide the rhizome

<SpeciesCard slug="java-fern" />

Anubias, bucephalandra and java fern propagate by **division**. Cut the rhizome into sections, each with a few leaves and its own roots, and [attach each piece](/guides/attaching-plants-to-driftwood-and-rock) to hardscape. Java fern also grows tiny daughter plants right on its leaf tips, snap these off once they have a few leaves and roots and attach them elsewhere. Never bury the rhizome of a division, it rots.

## Rosettes: remove the old outer leaves

<SpeciesCard slug="amazon-sword" />

Rosette plants like swords and crypts do not get "trimmed" so much as tidied: cut old, damaged or algae-covered outer leaves right off at the base, which lets light into the crown and keeps the plant fresh. They propagate by sending up plantlets on flower stalks or by splitting at the roots, which you separate and replant.

## Mosses: trim to branch

<SpeciesCard slug="java-moss" />

Moss trimmed flat branches and thickens into a dense carpet; moss left alone grows long and stringy. Trim it back to shape with scissors, and use the trimmings to start moss on new hardscape.

## Trimming habits

- **Sharp scissors.** Clean cuts heal better and crush less than blunt ones.
- **Little and often.** Small regular trims keep plants dense and the tank stable.
- **Remove the clippings.** Fish them out so they do not rot and feed algae.
- **Trim into good conditions.** A big trim right before a water change, and with stable [CO2](/equipment/co2-injection) and light, recovers fastest.
