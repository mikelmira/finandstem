## The short answer

There is no magic number of fish per litre, and the old "one inch per gallon" rule is a myth that ignores nearly everything that matters. Real stocking depends on four things: **bioload** (how much waste the fish produce, which depends on their adult size and body shape, not their length in the shop), **filtration and planting** (how much that tank can process), **schooling needs** (many fish must be kept in groups of six or more), and **behaviour and space** (territory, aggression, and room to swim). Work with those, understock rather than overstock, and use the [tank planner](/planner) for your exact volume instead of any per-gallon shortcut.

## Why the inch-per-gallon rule fails

"An inch of fish per gallon" is appealing because it is simple, and wrong because it is too simple. It breaks on every real tank:

- **Body shape.** A slim [neon tetra](/fish/neon-tetra) and a chunky fish can both be "two inches" while producing wildly different amounts of waste. Mass and metabolism matter, not length.
- **Adult size.** Fish are sold as juveniles. That "one inch" fish may grow to six. Always stock for the adult size.
- **Schooling.** The rule treats fish as interchangeable inches, but many species must be kept in groups to be healthy.
- **Territory and swimming space.** A single cichlid may need a whole tank's floor to itself; active swimmers need length regardless of "inches".

Use it, at most, as a loose sanity check, never as a plan.

## The four things that really decide stocking

### 1. Bioload

Every fish adds waste (ammonia) that the [cycle](/aquarium-cycling) and plants must process. Bigger, messier, hungrier fish add far more. A strong [filter](/equipment/filtration) and heavy planting raise how much waste a tank can handle, so a well-filtered, planted tank holds more than a bare one of the same size. Watch your [nitrate](/water-chemistry), a tank that needs constant large water changes to keep nitrate down is overstocked.

### 2. Schooling minimums

Shoaling and schooling species feel safe only in a group, usually **at least six** of their own kind, and more is better. Kept in ones and twos they turn stressed, drab and often nippy.

Plan around full schools of a few species rather than single specimens of many, which both looks better and keeps the fish healthy.

### 3. Territory and temperament

Some fish need space of their own. A pair of dwarf cichlids will claim and defend a patch of the floor, and boisterous or nippy fish need room and numbers to spread aggression.

Use the [compatibility tool](/compatibility) to check temperaments and territory before combining species, not after.

### 4. Swimming space and layout

A tank's footprint and length matter as much as its volume. Active swimmers need horizontal room; bottom dwellers need floor space; surface fish need surface area. A tall, narrow tank holds fewer active fish than a long, shallow one of the same litres.

## A sensible approach

- **Understock.** A lightly stocked tank is more stable, clearer, healthier and easier. Aim below the limit, not at it.
- **Build around schools.** A few full schools plus a centrepiece and a clean-up crew beats a crowd of singles.
- **Add a clean-up crew.** Shrimp, snails and algae grazers earn their keep with little bioload.
