## The short answer

A beautiful planted tank does not have to be expensive. The biggest savings come from going [low-tech](/guides/low-tech-vs-high-tech-planted-tank), which skips the whole CO2 system, and from choosing fast, hardy plants that multiply so you buy once and propagate the rest for free. Use inert sand or gravel with [root tabs](/glossary) instead of pricey aquasoil, source stone and wood locally, and start a shrimp colony that breeds itself. Spend your money where it matters, a decent [filter](/equipment/filtration) and [light](/equipment/lighting), and save it everywhere else.

## Where to save

### Skip CO2

CO2 is the single most expensive part of a planted tank to buy and run. Going [low-tech](/guides/low-tech-vs-high-tech-planted-tank) removes the cylinder, regulator, diffuser and drop checker entirely, lets you use a cheaper light, and cuts your fertiliser use. You lose carpets and vivid reds, but you keep a genuinely beautiful tank.

### Cheaper substrate

[Aquasoil](/substrates) grows plants faster, but plain inert sand or fine gravel is a fraction of the price and works well for most plants. Push a [root tab](/glossary) in near the heavy root feeders and they will be happy.

### Free and cheap plants

Buy fast, hardy plants that multiply, then let them do the work.

Hornwort grows like a weed and you can trim and replant the tops endlessly. Vallisneria spreads by runners into a jungle back wall. Java fern grows daughter plants you snap off and re-attach. A moss portion becomes a carpet over time.

Ask local aquarists too, planted-tank keepers are always giving away trimmings, and clubs and forums are full of cheap or free cuttings.

### A self-stocking clean-up crew

<SpeciesCard slug="cherry-shrimp" />

A small colony of [cherry shrimp](/guides/cherry-shrimp-care-guide) breeds readily in a stable tank, so ten shrimp become a hundred for nothing. Snails arrive free as hitchhikers and clean up waste.

## Where not to save

Two things are worth spending on, because cheating them causes the problems beginners waste money "fixing":

- **The filter.** An undersized or weak filter leaves waste and dead spots. Get one rated for four to six times your tank volume per hour. See [filtration](/equipment/filtration).
- **The light.** A poor light grows poor plants and often more [algae](/algae). A decent budget LED is a worthwhile buy.

Two cheap essentials you should never skip: a **dechlorinator** and a **liquid test kit**. The test kit is how you read the [cycle](/aquarium-cycling), and it is a few coins per test.

## Budget hardscape

[Stone and wood](/hardscape) are often the priciest single items in a shop, but they need not be. Many inert stones (basalt, slate, granite) can be collected locally and cleaned, as long as they are not calcareous (a splash of vinegar that fizzes means it will raise your hardness). Bogwood and manzanita are cheaper than branded aquascaping wood and do the same job. Arrange whatever you find using the [design principles](/aquascaping-design), good composition is free.

## A cheap tank that works

Put it together and a genuinely good budget tank looks like this: a modest tank, a decent LED and filter, inert sand with a few root tabs, some collected stone and wood, a heavy planting of hornwort, vallisneria, java fern, crypts and moss, and a starter colony of cherry shrimp. [Cycle it](/aquarium-cycling) properly, keep up a simple [maintenance routine](/aquarium-maintenance), and let the plants and shrimp multiply. Six months later it looks like it cost a fortune.
