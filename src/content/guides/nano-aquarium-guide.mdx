## The short answer

A nano aquarium, anything under about 40 litres, lets you build a complete planted scape on a desk or shelf. The catch is that a small water volume is less forgiving: temperature, pH and pollutants swing faster than in a big tank, so nano tanks reward stability and punish overstocking. Keep the stocking tiny and peaceful (a single small school of nano fish, or a shrimp colony), use a gentle filter and a small heater, plant heavily, and be disciplined about light feeding and small regular water changes. Do that and a nano tank is one of the most rewarding formats in the hobby.

## Small tank, big rules

The whole art of a nano tank is understanding that less water means less buffer. A heater failure, a missed water change, or a handful too much food hits a 20 litre tank far harder than a 200 litre one, because there is less water to dilute the problem. So the guiding principle is stability over ambition: understock, feed lightly, and keep a steady [maintenance routine](/aquarium-maintenance). Heavy planting helps a lot, because plants soak up waste and steady the tank.

## Equipment for a nano

- **Filter:** a small sponge filter or a nano internal, run gently so it does not blast tiny fish and shrimp around. See [filtration](/equipment/filtration).
- **Heater:** a small heater keeps temperature steady, which matters most in low volume. See [heaters](/equipment/heaters).
- **Light:** a nano LED is plenty. Keep the photoperiod to six to eight hours to hold [algae](/algae) off in the small volume.
- **Flow:** gentle and even. Read [circulation and flow](/equipment/circulation-and-flow).

## The best nano fish

Nano tanks demand genuinely small, peaceful fish. Stick to micro species and keep one small school, not a mix.

These stay tiny, school beautifully, and suit a nano's scale. Avoid anything that outgrows a few centimetres, and skip boisterous or nippy fish, which have nowhere to escape each other in a small tank. Use the [compatibility tool](/compatibility) and [tank planner](/planner) to keep it sensible.

## Or go shrimp-only

A shrimp-only nano is arguably the best use of a small tank. A colony of [cherry shrimp](/guides/cherry-shrimp-care-guide) turns a nano into a living, breeding, foraging little world, and needs no heater-hungry tropical fish.

<SpeciesCard slug="cherry-shrimp" />

Shrimp are sensitive to swings, so a stable nano suits them, and a heavily planted tank gives them the biofilm and cover they love.

## Plants for a nano

Scale matters. Small-leaved, slow plants keep a nano looking in proportion.

<SpeciesCard slug="bucephalandra" />

<SpeciesCard slug="monte-carlo" />

Bucephalandra and anubias on small pieces of hardscape, a fine carpet like Monte Carlo, and a little moss suit the scale. Big fast stems overwhelm a nano and need constant trimming. See [aquascaping design](/aquascaping-design) for composing a small scape, where restraint and open space matter even more.

## Keeping it stable

The nano keeper's habits: understock, feed a tiny pinch, change a small amount of water often (a fifth every week is easy at this size), and never let the heater or filter go unchecked. [Cycle it](/aquarium-cycling) fully before stocking, just like any tank, and watch the [numbers](/water-chemistry) closely, because they move fast in low volume.
