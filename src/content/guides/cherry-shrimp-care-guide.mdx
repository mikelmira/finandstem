## The short answer

Cherry shrimp (*Neocaridina davidi*) are the perfect first shrimp: hardy, colourful, and they breed themselves into a colony with no effort. Give them a stable, mature, planted tank with a steady [general hardness](/water-chemistry) around 6 to 8 GH, feed lightly, keep them away from fish that can eat them, and avoid copper, and they thrive and multiply. Almost every shrimp death traces back to instability, copper, or a failed moult from soft water. Get the water stable and keep it stable, and cherry shrimp are close to foolproof.

## Meet the cherry shrimp

Cherry shrimp are a selectively-bred colour form of *Neocaridina davidi*, and they come in many colours beyond red, blue, yellow, green and more, all the same species with identical care.

Because they are all one species, keeping two different colours together leads to muddy brown offspring over generations, so most keepers pick one colour per tank. For the difference between these hardy neocaridina and the fussier caridina shrimp, see [neocaridina vs caridina](/guides/neocaridina-vs-caridina-shrimp).

## Water parameters

Neocaridina are hardy but they hate swings. Aim for stable, moderately hard water:

- **pH:** 6.5 to 7.5
- **GH (general hardness):** 6 to 8, the most important number, because shrimp need calcium and magnesium to moult
- **KH:** 2 to 5
- **TDS:** roughly 150 to 250 ppm
- **Temperature:** 18 to 26 degrees, room temperature is often fine

Read the [water chemistry guide](/water-chemistry) to understand these, and remember: a steady "wrong" number beats a swinging "right" one. If your tap water is very soft, remineralise it to a stable GH rather than leaving it low, or you will see [failed moults](/diseases/shrimp-failed-molt).

## The tank

Cherry shrimp want a **mature, stable, planted tank**. Maturity matters because shrimp graze biofilm, the thin microbe layer that coats a settled tank, so an established tank of a few months feeds them for free. Set it up like any [planted tank](/guides/how-to-set-up-a-planted-aquarium), [cycle it fully](/aquarium-cycling), and let it mature before adding shrimp.

<SpeciesCard slug="java-moss" />

Plant heavily, and mosses especially, which trap biofilm and give shrimplets cover. Use a gentle [sponge filter](/equipment/filtration) so babies are not sucked in, and keep [flow](/equipment/circulation-and-flow) gentle.

## Feeding

Do not overfeed, it is the number one cause of shrimp trouble. A mature planted tank supplies most of their food as biofilm and soft algae. Supplement lightly, every couple of days, with:

- Blanched vegetables (courgette, spinach, leaves)
- A quality sinking shrimp food
- A calcium source (cuttlebone, a mineral stone, or a [remineraliser](/water-chemistry)) for strong moults

Remove uneaten food after a couple of hours.

## Tank mates

The safest cherry shrimp tank is a shrimp-only tank, because almost any fish eats the tiny shrimplets even if it ignores the adults. If you want fish, choose only small, peaceful nano species.

Chili rasbora, otocinclus and a small snail crew are about as safe as it gets, and even then some shrimplets get eaten, which just slows breeding. Check pairings with the [compatibility tool](/compatibility).

## Breeding

Breeding cherry shrimp is mostly about getting out of the way. Give a colony of ten or more a stable, mature, predator-light tank and they do the rest. A **berried** female carries a clutch of eggs under her tail (fanning them with her legs) for about a month, then releases miniature, fully-formed shrimp that fend for themselves immediately. No special setup, no larval stage. Just keep the water stable and the tank fed and the colony grows on its own.

## Why shrimp die, and how to prevent it

- **Unstable water.** Swings in GH, TDS or temperature, often from large or fast water changes, stress and kill shrimp. Change small amounts slowly.
- **Copper.** Lethal to shrimp, and hidden in many fish medications and some fertilisers. Check every product is shrimp-safe.
- **Failed moults.** Low GH means the new shell can't form, causing the [White Ring of Death](/diseases/shrimp-failed-molt). Keep GH steady and in range.
- **An immature or uncycled tank.** No biofilm, unstable water. Let the tank mature first.
