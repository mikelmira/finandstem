## The short answer

Otocinclus eat *diatoms* (soft brown algae) and biofilm. That is it. They will not touch the algae most aquarists actually want gone. Hair algae, black beard algae, green spot algae, and cyanobacteria are all off the menu. And otocinclus starve in a clean, newly-cycled tank because there is no biofilm yet to graze on. If you are buying otos to fix an algae problem in a two-month-old tank, do not. Buy them after three months of tank maturity, in groups of six or more, only when soft brown film is visible on the glass and leaves. Even then, supplement with Repashy Soilent Green and blanched zucchini, because they need calories beyond what algae alone provides. Otocinclus are excellent peaceful tankmates for shrimp and small fish, but they are a maintenance tool, not an algae solution. The actual fix for a hair algae or BBA outbreak is light and CO2 balance, not a hungry catfish.

## The algae types otos eat

Otocinclus are specialised browsers. Their mouths and gut are built for one job: scraping a thin film of soft material off smooth surfaces.

What they eat:

- **Diatoms.** Brown, dusty, slightly slimy film that appears on glass, hardscape, and plant leaves in young tanks. Diatoms are silica-loving algae that bloom heavily in the first two to three months of a tank's life, then taper off as the silica in the water gets consumed.
- **Biofilm.** The thin, invisible-to-the-eye microbial layer that coats every wet surface in a mature tank. Biofilm is what shrimp eat. It is also what otos eat as their primary food.
- **Soft green dust algae.** The faint green haze on glass that appears in mid-stage tanks. Otos will graze this when it is soft and recent. Once it hardens into green spot algae, it is too tough for them.

What they will not eat:

- **Hair algae.** Long stringy green filaments. Otos lack the jaw structure to bite through it.
- **Black beard algae (BBA).** Dark tufts on leaf edges and hardscape. Caused by CO2 instability. No fish or invertebrate eats it reliably except the Siamese algae eater (*Crossocheilus oblongus*), and even that one is hit or miss.
- **Green spot algae (GSA).** Small hard green dots on glass and leaves. Caused by phosphate deficiency. Scrape it manually.
- **Cyanobacteria.** Slimy blue-green sheets, often smelling like swamp. Not actually an algae. Treat with a blackout, an antibiotic, or by fixing flow and nitrate levels.

If you cannot tell which type of algae you have, take a clear photo against a white background and post to r/PlantedTank for identification before spending money on otos. The wrong purchase costs $30+ in fish that will die in two weeks.

## Why otos starve in new tanks

The species is built for grazing. Otocinclus eat constantly, all day, in small amounts. A wild oto in a forest stream picks at biofilm on roots and submerged wood for most of its waking hours.

A new tank does not have enough biofilm to feed a group of otos. The bacterial layer takes weeks to months to develop into something thick enough to graze meaningfully. Add otos in week two and they will rapidly graze through whatever biofilm exists, then have nothing to eat.

The signs of a starving oto:

- Sunken belly (concave between the pelvic and anal fins).
- Listless behaviour, hanging on a leaf without grazing.
- Lethargy in the mornings (a healthy oto is busy from lights-on).

By the time you notice these signs, the fish is usually too far gone to recover. Otos die from starvation in week one to three of being added to a too-young tank.

## When to actually buy them

The rule: at least three months of running planted tank, with visible biofilm or diatom film on glass and hardscape.

A tank that meets these criteria:

- Has been cycled and stable for 12+ weeks.
- Shows a faint brown film on glass that you can scrape off with a finger.
- Has driftwood with visible white-to-tan biofilm patches.
- Is moderately to heavily planted (more leaf surface area means more biofilm).

If your tank does not show diatom film, your tank cannot feed otos. Wait another month, or accept that you will need to supplement aggressively.

## How many to buy

<SpeciesCard slug="otocinclus" />

Six minimum. Otocinclus are obligate shoaling fish. Solo otos hide constantly and stop eating. A group of six in a 60 litre planted tank shows the natural behaviour: grazing in loose formation across glass, leaves, and hardscape, occasionally darting up in unison.

The species sold most commonly is *Otocinclus vittatus* (the "Common Oto"). Several closely-related species enter the trade under the same name (O. cocama "Zebra Oto", O. macrospilus, O. vestitus). For care purposes treat them as the same fish.

Buy them all from a single source on a single day. Acclimate slowly over 90+ minutes via drip. Lose one or two to acclimation stress as the baseline expectation.

## Supplementing their diet

Even in a tank with visible algae, supplement twice a week. Otos eat constantly and bare-tank biofilm production rarely keeps up.

Best supplemental foods:

- **Repashy Soilent Green.** Gel food formulated for plant-eating fish and invertebrates. Mix powder with hot water, pour into a mould or onto a plate, set in the fridge. Cut a small piece, drop into the tank. Otos and shrimp swarm it.
- **Blanched zucchini or courgette slices.** Microwave a 1cm-thick slice for 30 seconds, weight it down with a stainless skewer, leave overnight. Remove uneaten remains the next morning.
- **Blanched cucumber, spinach, or carrot.** Same method.
- **Hikari Algae Wafers.** Easy fallback if you do not want to deal with fresh veg.

A well-fed oto has a slightly rounded belly and is visibly active throughout the day.

## Better algae solutions for common problems

If you bought otos hoping to fix a specific algae problem, here is what actually works for each type.

**Hair algae:**

<SpeciesCard slug="amano-shrimp" />

A group of 6 to 10 Amano shrimp will demolish a hair algae bloom. The shrimp are large enough (4 to 5cm) to bite through filaments that otos and Neocaridina shrimp cannot manage. They are also better at it than any fish in the catalogue.

**Biofilm and diatoms:**

<SpeciesCard slug="cherry-shrimp" />

A cherry shrimp colony grazes the same biofilm otos eat, plus they multiply to match the food supply. Add 15 to 20 cherries to a planted tank and they will keep the glass and hardscape biofilm at a low maintained level forever.

**Black beard algae (BBA):**

Fix the CO2. BBA appears when CO2 drops or fluctuates. Add CO2 injection (or liquid carbon like Easy Carbo or Excel), turn down the lights by an hour per day for two weeks, and the BBA will recede. Manual removal of affected leaves accelerates the recovery.

**Green spot algae (GSA):**

Dose more phosphate. GSA blooms when PO4 is too low relative to NO3. A weekly phosphate dose (KH2PO4 at 1 to 2 ppm target) clears it over a month. The 2HR Aquarist [algae diagnosis guide](https://www.2hraquarist.com/blogs/algae-control) covers the chemistry in depth.

## What experienced keepers report

I have not kept otocinclus personally, so this section synthesises what the most credible community sources consistently report. The story is unusually unanimous across [Seriously Fish](https://www.seriouslyfish.com/species/otocinclus-vittatus/), [Aquarium Co-Op's otocinclus care guide](https://www.aquariumcoop.com/blogs/aquarium/otocinclus-care), and the [Tropica shop-fish notes](https://tropica.com/en/) on the species.

Three patterns come up again and again. First, otos that survive the first month tend to live for years (4 to 5 in a stable planted tank), but the first-month die-off rate is high enough that most keepers report losing one or two from a group of six within the acclimation window. Second, the group dynamic matters more than the algae diet: keepers who add a single oto consistently report unhappy fish; keepers who add six or more describe visible grazing activity within days and noticeably calmer behaviour overall. Third, the algae expectations almost always need to be reset. Otos work through diatoms and the soft brown film on glass and broad leaves fast. They do not touch hair algae, black brush algae, green spot algae on hardscape, or the cyanobacteria mat keepers most often want gone. Buyers who arrive with a clean mature tank and expect the otos to "find something" report the most starvation losses; buyers who add them to a tank with active brown diatoms report long-term success.

The recurring practical recommendations are: buy six minimum, quarantine before adding, and start supplementing with blanched zucchini or Repashy Soilent Green from week two on, regardless of what the tank looks like.
