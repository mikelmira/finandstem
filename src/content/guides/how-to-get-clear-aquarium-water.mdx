## The short answer

Cloudy water is not one problem, it is several, and the fix depends on the colour. **White or milky** is a bacterial bloom, normal in a new tank, and it clears itself if you leave it alone and feed lightly. **Green** is a free-floating algae bloom from too much light and nutrients, fixed with a blackout, a UV steriliser, or cutting light. **Yellow or brown** is tannins from driftwood, harmless and easy to reduce with carbon and water changes. **A fine grey dust** is disturbed substrate or debris, which the filter catches within hours. Work out which one you have, and the fix follows.

## White or milky water: a bacterial bloom

A white haze that makes the tank look like watered-down milk is a bacterial bloom, and it is the most common cloudiness in a new tank. When there is a surplus of nutrients and the surfaces are not yet fully colonised, bacteria multiply free-floating in the water column until they run out of food and settle down. It looks alarming and is completely harmless.

The fix is patience. It clears on its own in a few days to a couple of weeks. Crucially, **do not do big water changes**, which just reset the balance and prolong it. Feed the fish lightly, make sure the tank is properly [cycling](/aquarium-cycling), and wait. Heavy planting helps, because plants take up the nutrients the bacteria feed on.

## Green water: an algae bloom

Green, pea-soup water is a bloom of single-celled algae floating in the water column. It is driven by too much light and nutrients, and direct sunlight on the tank is a classic trigger. Ordinary [algae](/algae) grazers cannot touch it because it floats, so water changes barely help.

Three fixes work:

- **Blackout:** cover the tank completely for three days, no light at all. The algae starves and dies. Feed sparingly and it does not harm the fish.
- **UV steriliser:** clears green water within days by killing the free-floating cells as water passes through.
- **Fix the cause:** shorten the [photoperiod](/glossary), move the tank out of direct sun, and don't over-fertilise.

See the [green water page](/algae/green-water) in the algae hub for the full method.

## Yellow or brown water: tannins

Tea-coloured water is almost always tannins leaching from [driftwood](/hardscape). It is harmless, mildly softens and acidifies the water, and many keepers deliberately create this [blackwater](/guides/blackwater-aquarium-guide) look. To reduce it: pre-soak or boil new wood before it goes in, run activated carbon in the filter, and do water changes. It fades over weeks as the wood exhausts its tannins.

## Grey dust: debris and disturbed substrate

A fine grey or brown haze right after planting, rescaping or a substrate stir is just suspended particles. The [filter](/equipment/filtration) catches it within hours. A polishing filter pad or fine floss speeds it up. If it keeps happening, your [flow](/equipment/circulation-and-flow) may have a dead spot where detritus settles and re-suspends, or you are overfeeding.

## Keep it clear for good

Clear water is mostly a by-product of a balanced, well-kept tank:

- **Feed lightly.** Excess food is the fuel behind most cloudiness.
- **Keep up water changes and filter maintenance.** See the [maintenance routine](/aquarium-maintenance).
- **Plant heavily.** Plants out-compete the free-floating algae and bacteria.
- **Run a clean-up crew.** Shrimp, snails and algae grazers keep surfaces clean.
