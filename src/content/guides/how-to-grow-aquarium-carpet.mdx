## The short answer

A carpet is the hardest look in the hobby to fake, because it is mostly about light reaching the substrate and CO2 driving horizontal growth. Pick a carpeting plant that matches your setup: [Monte Carlo](/plants/monte-carlo) or [dwarf hairgrass](/plants/dwarf-hairgrass) for a hardier tank, [HC Cuba](/plants/hc-cuba) for the finest carpet if you run [CO2](/equipment/co2-injection). Plant it as many small portions spaced close together, give it strong light and, ideally, CO2, and either flood the tank or use the dry start method to get it rooted first. Then trim it to force it to spread sideways. Carpets reward patience and punish shortcuts with algae.

## Choose the right carpet plant

Carpets range from forgiving to fussy. Match the plant to how much light and CO2 you are willing to run.

<SpeciesCard slug="monte-carlo" />

**Monte Carlo** is the best all-rounder: small round leaves, a fresh green carpet, and enough tolerance to fill in slowly even without CO2 (faster with it). The usual first carpet.

<SpeciesCard slug="dwarf-hairgrass" />

**Dwarf hairgrass** gives a grassy lawn that spreads by runners. Hardy, and forgiving of moderate light, though a dense carpet still wants good light and CO2.

<SpeciesCard slug="marsilea-hirsuta" />

**Marsilea hirsuta** is the low-tech champion: very hardy and happy in modest light with no CO2. The catch is speed, it grows slowly, so a full carpet takes patience.

<SpeciesCard slug="hc-cuba" />

**HC Cuba (Hemianthus callitrichoides)** is the finest, most sought-after carpet, tiny leaves and a dense pearl of a lawn. It is also the most demanding: strong light and CO2 are effectively required, or it melts.

## Light and CO2 do the work

A carpet lives or dies by two things: enough light reaching all the way down to the substrate, and enough CO2 to make the plant grow sideways rather than up. When light and CO2 are strong, carpet plants creep horizontally and stay short and dense. When either runs short, they stretch upward reaching for light, thin out, and let [algae](/algae) into the gaps. This is why carpets are the flagship of the [high-tech tank](/guides/low-tech-vs-high-tech-planted-tank). See [lighting](/equipment/lighting) for PAR targets and [CO2 injection](/equipment/co2-injection) for the setup.

## The dry start method

The dry start is the reliable way to establish a carpet with no algae risk. You plant the carpet into damp [substrate](/substrates) with only a film of water, seal the tank with cling film or a lid to hold humidity, and grow the plants **emersed**, in air, for four to eight weeks. Emersed growth roots fast and dense, and because there is no standing water, algae cannot take hold. Once the carpet has knitted together, you flood the tank and the plants convert to underwater growth. Mist daily, keep it warm and lit, and be patient.

## Planting technique

However you grow it, plant small and plant dense.

1. Split the plant (or tissue-culture cup) into many small portions.
2. Using [tweezers](/glossary), push each portion a centimetre or two into damp substrate.
3. Space them a couple of centimetres apart across the whole foreground.
4. Keep the water shallow and the substrate damp while you work.

More, smaller plantlets close together fill in far faster than a few big clumps. Our [how to plant an aquarium](/guides/how-to-plant-an-aquarium) guide covers the full technique.

## Keeping it healthy

Once it is down, a carpet needs three things: a trim to force horizontal spread, a clean-up crew to keep detritus off it, and stable conditions.

<SpeciesCard slug="amano-shrimp" />

Trim the carpet as it thickens, which pushes it to spread rather than stack up and shade itself out. A crew of amano shrimp or a small algae-grazing fish keeps the surface clean, since detritus settling into a carpet is a fast route to [black beard algae](/algae/black-beard-algae). Watch for [deficiencies](/deficiencies) in fast carpets, and keep [flow](/equipment/circulation-and-flow) moving over the whole foreground so no dead spots form.
