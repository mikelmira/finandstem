## The short answer

This is the TL;DR. Open with the direct answer to the article's `targetQuery`
in 100–150 words. Lead with a sentence that names the species or topic and
states the conclusion. Crawlers and AI overviews extract from this first
paragraph more often than anywhere else on the page.

## Background

Here's the longer reasoning. Drop inline catalogue cards anywhere in the prose:

<SpeciesCard slug="neon-tetra" />

The card auto-pulls the photo, name, and origin from `src/data`. There's
nothing per-article to wire up. Cards work for any catalogue entry; pass
`category` only if a slug is ambiguous.

External links automatically get `target="_blank"`, `rel="noopener
noreferrer"`, and a little outbound icon. Authority domains (FishBase, GBIF,
Wikipedia, Tropica, Seriously Fish, 2HR Aquarist) keep `dofollow`; everything
else gets `rel="nofollow"`. Try one:
[FishBase entry for Paracheirodon innesi](https://www.fishbase.se/summary/Paracheirodon-innesi).

## Cross-link up to the pillar

Use `<PillarLink>` to recommend the pillar this guide hangs under. Every
guide must link up per Rule 7 of `seo/internal-linking-rules.md`:

<PillarLink href="/aquarium-fish-guide">
  the complete guide to aquarium fish for the planted tank
</PillarLink>

## What I've observed

This is the section that makes a guide rank for E-E-A-T's experience axis.
Write 2 to 4 paragraphs of first-hand observation. What surprised you, what
you'd do differently, parameters that worked in your specific water. Stock
SEO copy gets ignored here; specifics get cited.

## Closing thought

End with one sentence that points the reader either to the next logical
guide or to the compatibility tool. Reader knows where to go next, you keep
session depth high, search engines see internal-link equity move properly.
